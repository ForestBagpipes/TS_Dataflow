# AgentSpec, customisable runtime enforcement for LLM agents

ICSE 2026, preprint arXiv 2503.18666. Wang, Poskitt, Sun. **Prior work relative
to a 2026-09-24 submission by a wide margin.**

Distinct from Proof-Carrying Agent Actions, which is also in this directory and
is a different paper. AgentSpec is specification based runtime enforcement, and
Proof-Carrying is model agnostic runtime governance. Both cards are kept.

## What it does

A lightweight domain specific language for expressing runtime constraints on
agent behaviour. A rule is a trigger, a predicate and an enforcement mechanism.
At runtime the layer intercepts proposed actions and blocks those whose
predicate marks them unsafe, at millisecond overhead.

Evaluated on code execution agents, embodied agents and autonomous driving. It
prevents unsafe code executions in over 90 percent of cases, eliminates the
hazardous embodied actions tested, and reaches full compliance on the driving
rules. Rules generated by a language model reach 95.56 percent precision and
70.96 percent recall on the embodied setting.

## What it shares with this work

The architecture, more closely than any other paper here. A proposer emits an
action, a separate layer evaluates a predicate before the action takes effect,
and the layer is external to the proposer so its guarantee does not depend on
what the proposer learned. Our acceptance rule is the same construction, and
our non blocking property is the same concern their enforcement mechanisms
address by always leaving a permitted alternative.

Their trigger, predicate and enforcement triple maps onto ours directly: the
trigger is a proposed edit, the predicate is the conjunction of utility,
structural distance and risk conditions, and the enforcement is rollback to the
sandbox copy.

## Where it differs

**The predicate is assumed to be writable.** Their setting is one where a
domain expert can state what unsafe means. Do not delete outside this
directory. Do not exceed this speed. Do not execute a shell command containing
this pattern. The contribution is a language for expressing such rules and a
mechanism for enforcing them, and the correctness of any individual rule is the
user's responsibility.

We cannot write that predicate. Whether an edit to a window is harmful is not
expressible as a condition on the edit alone, which is why the natural approach
is to ask a model, and the finding is that the model answers wrongly over a
characterisable region. Our predicate is therefore partly learned and partly
calibrated rather than declared, and most of the work is establishing what it
can and cannot be built from.

**Rule quality is measured against intent, not against outcome.** Their 95.56
percent precision and 70.96 percent recall are for generated rules against
intended rules. Our equivalent question is whether the predicate that fires
corresponds to actual harm, and our answer is that the model based part of it
does not: corpus AUROC 0.468 with a bootstrap interval entirely below 0.5.

**Consequence for the paper.** AgentSpec is the closest architectural
precedent and we say so. What our work adds is not the enforcement layer, which
they and others have built, but the finding that in this domain the signal such
a layer would naturally enforce against is anti correlated with harm, and the
construction of a predicate that does not rely on it alone.
